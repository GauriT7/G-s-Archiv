# THE BODY, INC. — CUE CARDS

**How to use:** Print in landscape or view one card at a time on a phone/tablet. These are memory prompts, not a second script. Let yourself find the words live.

---

## CARD 01 — THE BODY WITH A DASHBOARD

**OPENING LINE:** “My body has submitted a quarterly performance review.”

- Wearable metrics: sleep / recovery / strain / cycle patterns
- When does paying attention become monitoring?
- Body as a place to live vs dashboard to improve
- Science + business + desire for control
- Promise: longevity, women's health, peptides, online authenticity

**LANDING LINE:** “The body, Inc. — the body as biology, the body as a business.”

## CARD 02 — CURIOSITY BEFORE CREDENTIALS

- Medicine path felt familiar: study → qualify → work → settle
- YouTube kid: Veritasium / Smarter Every Day / NileRed / Kurzgesagt
- Loved the rabbit hole, not just the answer
- Interest in the seams: medicine × technology × design × research
- Personal pattern: intense interest, then move on

**BEAT:** Don’t over-explain the family/medicine backstory. Be candid, not apologetic.

## CARD 03 — THE ONE-POST INSTAGRAM ACCOUNT

**ANECDOTE:** Optogenetics account. One post. “Full editorial calendar.”

- Explain simply: light-sensitive proteins let researchers control specific cells/neurons with light
- 2026 Nobel Prize: Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann, Georg Nagel
- Important: no credit claimed; personal coincidence only
- Interest outlasted the performance around the interest
- Online pressure: every curiosity → account / reel / carousel / brand

**LANDING LINE:** “Sometimes a thing can matter to you before it becomes anything useful to anyone else.”

## CARD 04 — LONGEVITY SCIENCE VS LONGEVITY INDUSTRY

- Wearables / protocols / supplements / red light / biological-age tests
- Distinguish research questions from products sold today
- Mechanism ≠ demonstrated benefit; biomarker shift ≠ longer life
- Stanford Medicine (Sep 2026): consumer biological-age tests can disagree; caution, not diagnosis
- Longevity vs healthspan: years lived with function, agency, independence
- Market sells control over uncertainty

**QUESTION:** What outcome was actually measured — and does it matter to a person's life?

## CARD 05 — WOMEN'S HEALTH IS NOT A NICHE

- Whole life course: menstrual → metabolic/cardiovascular → fertility/pregnancy → menopause/older age
- Formerly PCOS; renamed PMOS (polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome) in May 2026
- WHO's Jan 2026 fact sheet (before the name change): 10–13% prevalence; up to 70% undiagnosed
- WHO: robust menstruation research remains limited
- WHO: endometriosis affects ~190 million globally; diagnosis often delayed
- Symptoms normalised/dismissed; need better evidence and access
- Wearable cycle data ≠ diagnosis

**LANDING LINE:** “The point of better science is not to give us more graphs. It is to make people's lives better.”

## CARD 06 — MY WRIST SAYS ONE THING; MY BODY SAYS ANOTHER

- Tracking helps spot patterns and prompts attention
- Score can colour the day / become a story
- Look at trends + context, not one verdict
- Device estimates; doesn't know you better than you do
- More data isn't automatically better

**LANDING LINE:** “The point is to live in the body, not spend the whole day managing its dashboard.”

## CARD 07 — PEPTIDES & THE SHORTCUT ECONOMY

- Not all peptides are the same; legitimate medicines exist
- GLP-1 drugs ≠ all online “wellness peptides”
- BPC-157 / injectable GHK-Cu: FDA flags limited safety evidence/quality concerns for compounded use
- Retatrutide: investigational as of Oct 2026, not approved for public use
- Questions: human data? outcome? comparator? harms? purity? oversight? long-term safety?
- Curiosity is good; confidence must be earned

**AVOID:** Dosing, sourcing, protocols, or advice for self-injection. Keep this a critical discussion, not a how-to.

## CARD 08 — AUTHENTICITY & CONTENT CULTURE

- “Every private curiosity becomes a public identity”
- Sharing can build community; internet isn't inherently fake
- But content can replace the actual experience
- “What do I want to understand?” vs “How do I look like I understand it?”
- You collect beginnings; preparation can become avoidance
- Range is wonderful; without follow-through it becomes a museum of almosts
- Not every interest needs an audience / brand / monetisation

**LANDING LINE:** “You can be a beginner without announcing the beginning.”

## CARD 09 — WHO GETS THE FUTURE?

- Invention arrives before access is solved
- Access = diagnostics + travel + staff + follow-up + affordability + time
- India: pharmaceutical strengths alongside uneven access to continuous care
- AI/digital tools work only inside functioning care pathways
- Ask: who benefits, who is left out, what does it cost to maintain?

**LANDING LINE:** “The future is not fully here if it only works for the people who can afford to meet it.”

## CARD 10 — CLOSING

- Science expands the questions; say “I don't know yet” when it's true
- Better women's health evidence; longevity beyond expensive rituals; humane med-tech
- Personal: finish meaningful things, don't confuse beginning with making
- Body ≠ product roadmap; life ≠ dashboard; person ≠ finished profile

**FINAL LINE:** “The lab is open — but I'm trying to remember to actually live in the thing I'm studying.”

## QUICK FACT CHECK STRIP (KEEP BESIDE THE MIC)

- **PMOS / formerly PCOS:** international consensus name change on 12 May 2026. WHO's 22 Jan 2026 fact sheet (before renaming) estimated 10–13% prevalence among reproductive-age women; up to 70% may be undiagnosed. Explain both names to listeners.
- **Menstruation:** WHO says robust research is limited; disruptive, unusually painful, heavy, infrequent or unpredictable symptoms deserve assessment.
- **Endometriosis:** WHO fact sheet dated 15 Oct 2025: estimated 190 million affected globally.
- **Biological age:** Stanford Medicine, 23 Sep 2026: consumer tests may conflict and should be interpreted cautiously.
- **Peptides:** FDA identifies safety concerns for compounded BPC-157 and injectable GHK-Cu due to limited data/quality risks.
- **Retatrutide:** investigational as of Oct 2026; not approved for general public use.
- **Optogenetics:** Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2026, announced 5 Oct 2026.
