01 / BEFORE THE CANVASc. 40,000 YEARS AGO ↘
THE FIRST GALLERY
darkness · ochre · firelight

A MARK THAT OUTLIVED ITS MAKER

Before there
were museums.

Imagine entering a cave with only a flickering flame for company. On the walls: animals, handprints, marks made with minerals ground into powder and mixed with fat, water or other binders.

Red and yellow ochres came from iron-rich earth. Charcoal made black. The materials were local, imperfect, and astonishingly durable. These weren't paints bought off a shelf; they were pieces of the landscape, transformed by human hands.

Our first pigments were not invented. They were found.

SAMPLE 01 / FOUND IN THE GROUND

Ochre — the colour of earth

Iron-rich clay was collected, dried, ground and sifted into a fine powder. Mix it with water, animal fat or another binder and earth becomes paint. Ochres are among the oldest pigments humans have used.

03 / COLOUR & POWERTHE WORLD GETS SMALLER ↘

A PIGMENT COULD COST A FORTUNE

When colour
became currency.

Some colours were so difficult to obtain that they became symbols of devotion, wealth and power. Lapis lazuli travelled from mines in what is now Afghanistan; ground into ultramarine, it could be more precious than gold in Renaissance Europe.

Across trade routes, dyes and pigments moved with textiles, manuscripts, merchants and empires. In South Asia, mineral pigments, plant dyes and gold were used in the layered visual worlds of miniature painting, textiles and illuminated manuscripts.

04 / IMPRESSIONISM & AFTERLOOK AT THE LIGHT ↘

A DIFFERENT WAY OF SEEING

Let the
light in.

In nineteenth-century France, Impressionist painters took their work outdoors and paid close attention to changing light, atmosphere and the fleeting feeling of a moment. Instead of smoothing every mark away, they often let separate brushstrokes remain visible.

Portable paint tubes made it easier to work outside. New synthetic pigments expanded the palette. The result wasn't simply a new look — it was a new way to notice.

The brushstroke became part of the story.

Colour is physical — wavelengths of light interpreted by our eyes — but its meaning is learned, inherited, traded and reinvented. Select a colour to follow a few of its many lives.

RED · EARTH, BLOOD, CEREMONY

Red has never been quiet.

From iron-rich ochre on cave walls to cochineal-dyed cloth, red has carried many meanings: vitality, danger, devotion, celebration and power. The material changes; the colour keeps finding ways to be noticed.
